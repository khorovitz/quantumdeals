      Methodology, baselines & references ▸

      What a rating means

      Conviction = Σ (rating ÷ 5 × weight), with weights summing to 100. The baseline is a rating of 3 on every dimension — the bar a fundable deep-tech startup is expected to clear — which yields a conviction of 60. Above 3 marks genuine strength; below 3 marks a real gap. This mirrors standard venture diligence, which weights team, market, technology, moat, and terms rather than scoring them equally.

      Attractiveness tiers

      Tier A · 75–100High conviction — clears the bar for an active strategic move.
      Tier B · 60–74Promising — above baseline; act selectively, watch the gaps.
      Tier C · 45–59Marginal — below baseline; needs de-risking before commitment.
      Tier D · 0–44Below bar — the venture case isn't there yet.

      The three risks gate the posture, not the score

      Deep-tech ventures fail on three distinct axes — science risk (does the core claim hold), execution risk (can this team build it), and market risk (will anyone pay, and when). They are rated separately and shape the type of move: high, unmitigated science risk on an unproven primitive caps even a high score at a staged option rather than an acquisition.

      Posture logic (neutral vantage)

      This reads from a general acquirer/investor seat, not one company's roadmap. Foundational hardware is capital-intensive and winner-uncertain, so it usually favors a partnership or staged option over an early vertical build; systems, software, and security layers tend to be more defensible and faster to value, which can support a build or acquisition when fit, conviction, and stage align. "Strategic fit" is fit to whatever thesis you hold.
